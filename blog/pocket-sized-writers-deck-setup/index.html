My Pocket-Sized Writer's Deck Setup (No 3D Printer Required)

How I turned an e-reader into a pocket writer's deck with MicroSlate, a folding Bluetooth keyboard and a magnetic stand. No 3D printer needed.

Updated November 15, 2026 · First published April 21, 2026 · By Josh

Disclosure: This post contains affiliate links. If you buy through these links, I may earn a small commission at no extra cost to you.

I bought it for the books

I bought the Xteink X4 because I wanted a book in my pocket everywhere I went: a small e-reader that holds my whole library and doesn't weigh down my bag.

Then I watched people build their own firmware for it, and I realized this little device would make an excellent writer's deck. A dedicated writing tool that fits in a pocket and costs a fraction of what the big brands charge.

So I built one. That project became MicroSlate.

It works out of the box

This setup needs no assembly and no 3D printer. Every piece ships ready to use, and the whole kit slips into a jacket pocket. Here's what's in mine:

Logitech Keys-to-Go 2

A slim Bluetooth keyboard that pairs with MicroSlate in seconds. It's thin, has good key travel for its size and lasts months on a charge. It fits in the same pocket as the X4.

View on Amazon →

Magnetic fold-up stand

A small magnetic stand that holds the X4 at a comfortable writing angle. It folds flat and weighs almost nothing, so you won't notice it in a bag until you need it.

View on Amazon →

Why it works as a writing tool

  • It really fits in a pocket. I take it to the café, the park and the couch.
  • It only writes. MicroSlate opens to your notes, so there's nothing else to tap on.
  • E-ink is easy on the eyes. It reads like paper and stays sharp in sunlight.
  • You pay once. You buy the hardware, and MicroSlate is free.
  • It works offline. Notes save to the microSD card.

Update: MicroSlate 3.0

MicroSlate has grown since I wrote this. Version 3.0 is built for two e-paper devices from Seeed Studio:

  • reTerminal Sticky: pocket-sized, with a 3.97" touch screen and a magnetic back.
  • reTerminal E1001: a 7.5" screen for your desk that shows whole paragraphs at once.

The Xteink X4 Pro has experimental support. If you own the original X4 like the one in my photo, MicroSlate 2.1 still runs on it alongside the CrossPoint reader.

3.0 also brings 32 keyboard layouts, notes you can send to your phone over Wi-Fi, and cloud sync with OneDrive, Google Drive or Dropbox.
