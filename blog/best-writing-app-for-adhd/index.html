Writing Tools for ADHD: Apps That Keep You on the Page

Writing with ADHD means fighting distractions. Here are the writing apps that help, from TypeSlate and Cold Turkey Writer to FocusWriter and 750words.

Updated November 15, 2026 · First published July 10, 2025 · By Josh

Writing with ADHD means fighting distractions all the time. You switch tabs mid-sentence, spend an hour researching a simple idea, or rewrite the first line until it's perfect and never reach the second.

A distraction-free writing app won't fix that on its own, but the right one can be the difference between a page and an empty file. Here are the tools that help, and who each one suits. Full disclosure: I built the first one.

1. TypeSlate: the most structure

TypeSlate is a fullscreen writing app that keeps you in until you hit a word count or timer goal. If tab-hopping is your problem, it's the strictest tool here.

What helps with ADHD:

  • Fullscreen lock: it stays fullscreen until your word or time goal is met.
  • Paste blocking: you can't paste text in from elsewhere, so a "research" break can't fill your word count.
  • Offline: no account and no cloud. Your words stay on your computer.
  • Free: there's nothing to buy.

Why it works: it puts friction in the right places. You make fewer decisions and have fewer ways out, so you stay in the flow longer. It's especially good for building a daily journaling habit.

2. Cold Turkey Writer: total lockdown

Think of Cold Turkey Writer as TypeSlate's older sibling. It pioneered the text editor that locks your whole screen until you meet your goal.

  • Locks you out of every other app
  • Can turn off backspace and delete for stream-of-consciousness writing
  • Works without a cloud account

The downsides: the design feels dated, and some people find the lockdown so intense it makes them anxious. Use it if you want zero escape and momentum matters more to you than editing.

3. FocusWriter: calm and gentle

FocusWriter suits writers who need a calm screen and a gentle nudge more than a lock.

  • Custom backgrounds and themes
  • Daily goals by word count or time
  • Fullscreen writing with a hidden interface
  • Free and open source

It's a good fit for journalers, students and early drafts.

4. 750words.com: streaks and stats

750words.com runs in your browser and rewards you for showing up. It tracks your daily words, awards streak badges and shows stats about your writing. If streaks motivate you, it works well. If you want to write offline or in fullscreen, look elsewhere.

5. iA Writer, Ulysses and Scrivener: good tools, mixed for ADHD

These popular apps have clean screens, but their long feature lists can overwhelm.

  • iA Writer is great for Markdown and has a focus mode.
  • Ulysses is powerful, but it's a subscription and has a lot to learn.
  • Scrivener is made for long projects and is heavy on menus and structure.

Pick one of these if you already have a steady writing habit or need to manage a big project.

Bonus: a device that only writes

If the computer itself is the distraction, take it out of the picture. A writer's deck is a small device that does nothing but write. Which one should you choose?

  • Want the most structure? Try TypeSlate.
  • Want a total lockdown? Try Cold Turkey Writer.
  • Want a calm, gentle push? Try FocusWriter.
  • Motivated by streaks? Try 750words.com.
  • Want to leave the computer behind? Try MicroSlate.

One tip: pair any of these with a timer, headphones and a focus playlist. ADHD is a different path to creativity, not the end of it. The right tool can make writing feel like a practice instead of a fight.
