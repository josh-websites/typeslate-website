The Morning Pages App I Built for Myself

I write my morning pages between 5 and 7 AM, before the kids wake up. Here's the app I built to make the habit stick, and how I use it.

Updated November 15, 2026 · First published July 10, 2025 · By Josh

Confession: I didn't finish The Artist's Way.

The book is valuable. But I picked it up expecting a book about creativity, and I found a 12-week workbook with assignments and solo artist dates.

I'm a preschool teacher and a dad of two. Taking myself out on creative dates is a comedy routine. The only time I get to myself is from 5:00 to 7:00 AM, before breakfast and daycare drop-off.

That quiet window changed my life, thanks to the one habit I kept from the book: morning pages.

What morning pages are

The idea is simple. First thing in the morning, write about 750 words of whatever is in your head. It clears the clutter and gets the ideas moving.

The book says to write them by hand. I don't handwrite. I type everything.

Why Word didn't work

I started with a Word document called "Brain Purge." Every morning I scrolled to the bottom to find where I'd left off, did math to work out my word count, and tried to ignore autocorrect and the formatting buttons.

It worked, barely, and I didn't enjoy it. Morning pages need unfiltered focus, and Word kept pulling me out of it.

So I built TypeSlate

Write your pages on e-paper

MicroSlate turns a small e-paper screen and a Bluetooth keyboard into a writing machine you can take anywhere.

Meet MicroSlate

That frustration is why I built TypeSlate, a fullscreen writing app that counts your words and keeps you writing until you hit your goal.

Every morning I open it, set my goal to 750 words and press start. The screen goes dark and I write until the progress bar fills.

Here's what makes it work for morning pages:

  • It stays fullscreen until you reach your word count.
  • The page is the only thing on screen. Spellcheck is off, so you can't stop to fix things.
  • It counts the words for you, so there's no math and no scrolling.
  • Your pages stay on your computer.
  • It's free.

Why not a journaling app?

Evernote, Obsidian and Google Docs want to organize your thoughts with tags, folders and sync. Morning pages don't need organizing. They need writing. TypeSlate does that one job, and it does it better than anything else I've used.

It suits:

  • writers who start the day with a brain dump
  • students and professionals who want a clearer head
  • journalers who want privacy and a daily habit
  • anyone who wants a quiet start to the morning

What I do with the pages afterward

When I finish, TypeSlate copies the text to my clipboard. Some days I paste it into ChatGPT to ask follow-up questions or to find a blog post idea hiding in a rant. That isn't morning pages by the book, but it works for me. If your pages are private, think about where you paste them.

Does the habit stick?

Since I built TypeSlate I've written over 100,000 words, most of them before breakfast. The routine helps me sort out ideas, notice patterns and keep writing every day.

If morning pages haven't stuck for you yet, give TypeSlate a try. It's free on the Microsoft Store.
