A Writing Tool That Doesn't Want to Write for You

AI is in every writing tool now. Here's why I built TypeSlate without it, and why the writing itself should stay human.

Updated November 15, 2026 · First published July 10, 2025 · By Josh

Oh look, another shitty GPT wrapper

They're everywhere. Every program is getting AI shoehorned in. AI toilets already exist. AI toothbrushes are so 2024.

Writing tools aren't immune. As I edit this in Word, the Copilot icon drifts beside the line, following me down the page and begging to finish my thoughts for me.

We're letting the bots do our reps

I'll admit I'm a heavy AI user. I use ChatGPT at work, and ChatGPT and Claude for personal projects. But writing is different. Writing is a practice, almost a religious one. You wouldn't ask an AI to run a mile for you so you could get in shape.

You learn to write by putting in the reps. If we let AI into every corner of our lives, the creative corners included, we'll end up consuming whatever the algorithms hand us and never asking why.

Some tools should stay human

TypeSlate is one of them.

I'm not against AI. AI helped me build this program and this website. It even helped me brainstorm this post. Then I deleted everything it wrote and wrote what I wanted to say, because writing should be messy, imperfect and human. That's also why my first drafts happen at 5 AM, before I can talk myself out of them.

Spend any time in writing circles on Reddit and you'll see open hostility to AI in every part of the business. You'll also see AI champions who are mostly trying to get rich quick.

I'm on the fence

Artists should get paid. I'm also broke, and when I started this blog I made the images with ChatGPT because it was quick and easy. I've since replaced them with plain title cards. Canva templates aren't a clean fix either, because Canva is full of AI too.

What I can say is that TypeSlate has no AI in it. You open it and write. That simplicity is what makes it valuable, and it's free.

What TypeSlate leaves out

TypeSlate won't suggest your next word or analyze your tone, and it never sends your writing to a server where it could train a model. Instead you get:

  • a fullscreen mode that keeps you in until you hit your goal
  • offline writing, with no account and no cloud
  • paste blocking, so you can't cheat your own goal
  • writing stats that stay on your computer, for your own growth

Most tools are chasing AI and automation. TypeSlate chases focus. It's a place to write, and the writing is up to you.

I built MicroSlate, my e-paper writer's deck firmware, the same way. It has a keyboard, a page and no AI.

Doing the work yourself is the point

There's something radical about doing the work yourself. With TypeSlate, every word on the page is yours.
